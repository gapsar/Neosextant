# Neosextant Observation Guide

## Taking Good Observations
- **Keep the phone steady:** Motion blur is the enemy of star detection. Use a steady hand.
- **Center the horizon:** Ensure the horizon line is visible in the lower half of your screen, as the pitch sensor depends on it.
- **Spread them out:** For the best position fix, capture stars in different directions (e.g., one North, one South-East, one West). A good spread in azimuth reduces geometric dilution of precision.
- **Time your shots:** The sky should be dark enough for stars to be visible, but capturing during nautical twilight can help keep the horizon visible.

## Calibration
- **Sensor Calibration:** Your phone's internal sensors can suffer from magnetic interference or bias. If the app consistently fails to get a fix, try placing the phone on various resting angles and using the "Sensor Calibration" button.
- **Horizon Offset:** The camera lens might not be perfectly parallel to the device's internal IMU axes. Use the Horizon Calibration tool to align the reticle with the true sea horizon. This establishes a baseline dip offset.
